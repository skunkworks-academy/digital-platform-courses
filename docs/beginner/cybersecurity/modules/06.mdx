# Module 6: Responding to incidents and building good habits

**Estimated time:** 45–70 minutes. **Outcome:** The response is timely, avoids exposing credentials, escalates appropriately and documents recovery verification.

## Core concepts and why they matter

An incident response cycle includes preparation, detection, initial containment, evidence preservation, recovery and lessons learned. A learner should know whom to contact, how to disconnect a clearly compromised device when instructed, and why deleting evidence or improvising a forensic investigation can cause harm. Do not send suspect files to colleagues. A plan should cover account recovery, identity verification, backup restoration and external notifications according to organisational policy. Track objective signals rather than rumours.

## Worked workplace scenario

A fictional staff member enters credentials into a convincing imitation website. Create an escalation note and outline immediate safe steps: report, change credentials via the real site, revoke sessions, enable MFA and have authorised IT inspect account activity.

## Guided hands-on application

1. Prepare a one-page incident card with contact, classification, time, affected account and safe next actions.
2. Write a timeline of a simulated phishing incident from receipt through recovery.
3. Identify which evidence should be recorded without unnecessarily storing the malicious attachment.
4. Present three weekly routines that materially reduce cyber risk.

## Verification and failure test

The response is timely, avoids exposing credentials, escalates appropriately and documents recovery verification. Create a table identifying expected result, actual result, date, supporting evidence and next action. Use one deliberately invalid or unexpected input to confirm the control does not silently fail.

:::warning Safety and ethics
Do not promise that changing a password resolves all account compromise; inspect sessions and delegated access.
:::
