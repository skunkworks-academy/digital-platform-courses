# Module 1: Cyber threats, assets and everyday risk

**Estimated time:** 45–70 minutes. **Outcome:** A risk register exists with asset, event, likelihood, consequence, owner, control and residual risk.

## Core concepts and why they matter

Cybersecurity protects confidentiality (only appropriate people see information), integrity (records are accurate) and availability (systems function when needed). Identify assets—accounts, devices, files, networks and business reputation—and threats such as malware, scams, loss, misconfiguration and physical theft. A vulnerability is a weakness; risk depends on likelihood and impact, not the number of tools installed. Defence in depth layers controls so that one failure does not reveal everything.

## Worked workplace scenario

A fictional clinic stores appointments on a laptop. A stolen laptop creates confidentiality risk; an untested backup creates availability risk. Explain why encryption and backups solve different problems.

## Guided hands-on application

1. List five information assets and their owners for a fictional microbusiness.
2. Rate likelihood and impact (low/medium/high) for phishing, lost laptop, password reuse and interrupted Internet service.
3. Match each risk with a preventive, detective and recovery control.
4. Explain which action should be taken first if the most damaging incident occurs.

## Verification and failure test

A risk register exists with asset, event, likelihood, consequence, owner, control and residual risk. Create a table identifying expected result, actual result, date, supporting evidence and next action. Use one deliberately invalid or unexpected input to confirm the control does not silently fail.

:::warning Safety and ethics
Do not attempt to scan, break into, or test other people's systems without written authorisation.
:::
