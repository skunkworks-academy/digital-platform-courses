# Module 4: Documents, summaries and communication

**Time:** about 45–60 minutes. **Outcome:** The output retains all essential facts and never invents task ownership, deadlines or approvals.

## Learn: core concepts

AI can condense long material, extract action items, restructure notes and suggest accessible writing. The human author must check that a summary preserves decisions, dates, owners and caveats. A safe document workflow starts with data classification: public, internal, confidential or regulated. Only approved content may be uploaded. Apply accessible structure with descriptive headings, concise sentences, alt-text drafted then reviewed and explicit next actions.

## Realistic workplace scenario

Given fictional meeting notes containing six actions, a prompt requests an action register with owner, deadline, dependency and unresolved question. Blank owner fields remain 'unassigned' rather than being fabricated.

## Guided activity

1. Create one page of fictional meeting notes with decisions, open questions and five tasks.
2. Prompt for a 120-word summary plus a four-column action register.
3. Compare each extracted fact with the source; highlight omissions or invented commitments.
4. Rewrite one stakeholder email and assess tone, accuracy and accessibility with a checklist.

## Verify your result

The output retains all essential facts and never invents task ownership, deadlines or approvals. Produce a brief validation table with columns **expected result**, **actual result**, **evidence**, **decision** and **remediation**. The observed result must match the stated purpose without accidental disclosure or unapproved action.

:::warning Responsible practice
Summaries compress; they can lose risk notes. Keep an auditable link to the original authorized document.
:::

## Extended learning: accurate summaries and effective communications

### Summarise information without changing obligations

A summary should preserve the difference between **decision, suggestion, action item and unresolved question**. A meeting discussion about possibly launching training in November is not evidence that a November launch was approved. Likewise, an AI-produced email draft should not silently add terms, prices, concessions or recipients.

An effective summary has four sections: **decisions confirmed**, **actions with verified owners**, **outstanding questions**, and **source references**. A reader should be able to find each material statement in the original notes. For email, include a clear subject, reason for writing, specific request and next step.

### Worked example: meeting-to-action workflow

1. Use these fictional notes: “The group reviewed learner feedback. Maria will draft the course guide. A launch month was discussed but not approved. The pricing team must verify fees.”
2. Create a summary with the four sections above.
3. Assign Maria only the drafting action; do not assign ownership of the pricing review to an unnamed person.
4. Write a short follow-up email requesting **confirmation** of the launch month and approved price.
5. Compare the summary with the notes, checking every date, owner and decision verb.
6. Produce an accessible version with descriptive headings and no information conveyed only through colour.

### Quality-control checklist

| Aspect | Pass standard |
|---|---|
| Completeness | All decisions, actions and open questions appear |
| Accuracy | No invented approvals, owners, deadlines or numbers |
| Privacy | Recipients and included information are authorised |
| Accessibility | Plain language, clear headings and meaningful links |
| Traceability | Notes or approved source can be identified |

**Challenge:** Deliberately introduce one invented deadline into a fictional AI summary. Explain how your review detected and corrected it before the document reached the recipient.
