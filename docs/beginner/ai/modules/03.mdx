# Module 3: Fact-checking and responsible research

**Time:** about 45–60 minutes. **Outcome:** A corrected brief must clearly separate observations, assumptions, and unsupported claims; every consequential fact has traceable evidence.

## Learn: core concepts

Language models may hallucinate fabricated references, policy details, quotations and statistics. An answer needs a verifiable source and a relevant publication date where facts are changing. Separate first-party documentation, reputable secondary analysis and unsupported opinion. Quotes, prices and regulatory obligations should be checked against originals. When researching a rapidly changing technology, record the date of verification and the product version.

## Realistic workplace scenario

A model claims a particular cloud service is free worldwide. The reviewer opens the current vendor pricing page, verifies regional eligibility and records terms before quoting a customer.

## Guided activity

1. Ask an approved AI assistant to list three features of a named workplace tool and cite official documentation.
2. Open the corresponding official vendor pages manually, then mark each assertion verified, contradicted or unknown.
3. Correct the generated summary and add source URL and checked date to each material claim.
4. Document two circumstances in which you would decline to publish the answer.

## Verify your result

A corrected brief must clearly separate observations, assumptions, and unsupported claims; every consequential fact has traceable evidence. Produce a brief validation table with columns **expected result**, **actual result**, **evidence**, **decision** and **remediation**. The observed result must match the stated purpose without accidental disclosure or unapproved action.

:::warning Responsible practice
Do not treat a plausible-looking URL, fabricated citation or AI-generated confidence score as independent verification.
:::

## Self-check and feedback

1. What must you record when confirming a time-sensitive claim?
2. What should happen when a source cannot be verified?

<details>
  <summary>Read feedback after answering</summary>

The answer must demonstrate the principles taught above: verification against evidence, safe handling, defined responsibility and a reproducible process. Revisit the scenario if you cannot show how a reviewer would confirm the result independently.

</details>
