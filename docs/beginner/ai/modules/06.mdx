# Module 6: Building a safe personal AI workflow

**Time:** about 45–60 minutes. **Outcome:** The workflow names a human owner, evidence, exception path, privacy boundary and measurable improvement target.

## Learn: core concepts

A workflow includes an input, transformation, review checkpoint, output, owner and feedback loop. Start with one small task before considering automation. Create a repeatable prompt template and a quality rubric. Put mandatory human approval on consequential actions. Data minimisation, appropriate retention, consent, copyright and company policy are design inputs—not optional afterthoughts. Evaluate benefit by comparing total time before and after, including verification and rework.

## Realistic workplace scenario

A sales representative uses AI to draft three fictional customer follow-ups, manually checks every claim, then sends only messages approved in a customer relationship management process.

## Guided activity

1. Choose one low-risk repetitive task and record current handling time and error frequency for five simulated examples.
2. Design the workflow using a trigger, permissible input, prompt template, validation checklist, authorized approver and archived output.
3. Pilot three examples and calculate preparation time plus review time, not just time saved in drafting.
4. Write a stop rule for unexpected disclosure, fabricated claims or inconsistent output.

## Verify your result

The workflow names a human owner, evidence, exception path, privacy boundary and measurable improvement target. Produce a brief validation table with columns **expected result**, **actual result**, **evidence**, **decision** and **remediation**. The observed result must match the stated purpose without accidental disclosure or unapproved action.

:::warning Responsible practice
Do not confuse 'fully automated' with 'safe'. A human can approve work before any external communication or irreversible action.
:::

## Extended learning: safe personal AI operating procedure

### Treat the workflow as a controlled process

A reliable workflow needs a **trigger, approved inputs, transformation, validation, decision gate, output and recovery procedure**. Safety cannot rely on the model being instructed to be careful. Consequential actions such as sending a customer quote, editing an official register or providing regulated advice require controls outside the generative response.

### Worked example: a training enquiry assistant

The fictional workflow is designed to draft answers without sending them automatically:

1. **Intake:** Receive a permitted enquiry in an authorised mailbox.
2. **Classification:** Identify requested courses and whether personal information is present.
3. **Minimal input:** Provide only the necessary, non-sensitive facts and approved catalogue data to the assistant.
4. **Generation:** Produce a draft plus a list of uncertain items such as unconfirmed prices.
5. **Review gate:** Staff compare the output against the approved course catalogue and pricing record.
6. **Decision:** An authorised person approves, edits or rejects the draft.
7. **Record:** Keep a minimal audit note of source, decision and corrections without storing secrets.

### Define failure handling

| Failure | Required response |
|---|---|
| AI invents a discount | Remove claim and confirm price with authorised owner |
| Customer submits sensitive documents | Do not copy them into an unapproved tool; follow policy |
| Source catalogue unavailable | Pause the quote instead of guessing |
| Reviewer cannot verify a date | Mark as pending and request confirmation |

Measure success using **correction rate, turnaround time and escalation quality**, not just the number of messages generated.

**Capstone preparation:** Draw the workflow as a diagram with a visible human approval decision. Add one deliberately malformed enquiry and describe how the process rejects or escalates it.
