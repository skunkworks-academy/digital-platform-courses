# Module 1: Understanding AI and what it can do

**Time:** about 45–60 minutes. **Outcome:** A credible table distinguishes generation from factual verification and names a person responsible for publishing or acting.

## Learn: core concepts

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the broad field of making systems perform tasks that normally require human cognitive skills. Machine learning learns statistical patterns from data. Generative AI produces new text, images or other media based on patterns and instructions. A language model predicts plausible sequences; it does not possess an internal truth database. A chatbot is an interface, not a guarantee of expertise. Distinguish a general-purpose model from a company-approved assistant connected to enterprise information. The latter may apply different access and retention controls.

## Realistic workplace scenario

A recruitment assistant drafts interview questions from a role description. It should not decide who is qualified or infer protected characteristics. The human reviewer checks that criteria are job-related and inclusive.

## Guided activity

1. Create a two-column table headed 'Task' and 'Human accountability'. Include summarising a memo, checking facts, creating an illustration and deciding a loan.
2. For each task identify information the model would need, the level of confidentiality and a realistic failure.
3. Classify each task as low, medium or high consequence and decide whether AI should draft, advise or be excluded.
4. Write a one-sentence disclosure appropriate for AI-assisted content in your workplace.

## Verify your result

A credible table distinguishes generation from factual verification and names a person responsible for publishing or acting. Produce a brief validation table with columns **expected result**, **actual result**, **evidence**, **decision** and **remediation**. The observed result must match the stated purpose without accidental disclosure or unapproved action.

:::warning Responsible practice
Fluent output is not proof of truth. Never paste company passwords, customer databases or private contracts into an unapproved service.
:::

## Extended learning: AI systems, limitations and human accountability

### Distinguish the technology from the interface

Artificial intelligence is an umbrella term; machine learning is a family of approaches that infer patterns from examples, while generative AI creates candidate content. A language-model chatbot presents generated responses in a conversational interface. It may also be connected to search, retrieval or tools, but **the presence of a citation or confident wording does not guarantee correctness**. Ask which information came from the model, from an approved source and from a human decision.

Consider four office tasks:

| Task | Appropriate AI contribution | Required human check |
|---|---|---|
| Summarise a fictional meeting | Produce a draft summary | Compare decisions, owners and dates with original notes |
| Create interview questions | Draft role-related prompts | Review fairness, relevance and approved hiring criteria |
| Calculate a supplier total | Explain the calculation | Recalculate the numbers independently |
| Approve a loan | None without approved controls and policy | An authorised decision-maker applies the formal process |

### Worked example: risk-classify an AI request

1. **Describe the decision.** A team wants a chatbot to answer routine training questions.
2. **Inspect the inputs.** Public course descriptions are permitted; client identity documents and passwords are not.
3. **Classify the consequence.** A formatting suggestion is low consequence. A confirmed examination date, price or refund promise may create obligations.
4. **Set a review threshold.** Draft answers may be prepared automatically, but prices, eligibility and dates must come from a verified record.
5. **Assign the owner.** Name the person who checks the source and authorises sending the message.

### Apply and reflect

Write three examples of AI assistance in your own environment and document who verifies the output. For each, identify a realistic failure, how someone would detect it, and when to stop the workflow. Explain why generating a plausible response and retrieving a verified fact are different capabilities.
