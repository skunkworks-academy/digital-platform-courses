# Final capstone: office productivity and reporting portfolio

## Business scenario

A fictional training organisation with eight staff members wants to improve the consistency of internal updates and monthly expense reporting. Staff need clear documents, valid spreadsheet totals, accessible visuals, safe sharing and approval checks before external delivery.

## Required outputs

1. Create a named folder structure, versioning convention and information-classification note.
2. Build a six-to-ten-row expense or ticket workbook with valid data types, calculations and one absolute-reference example.
3. Implement at least three functions, such as SUM, AVERAGE, IF or COUNTIF, and manually cross-check key results.
4. Add a labelled chart and a short written report distinguishing observations and assumptions.
5. Draft an email requesting manager approval and a meeting agenda with a time zone and intended decision.
6. Document sharing roles for internal editors and external readers, with explicit prevention of unrestricted links.
7. Demonstrate three positive and three negative tests, one covering corrupted input and one unauthorized sharing attempt.
8. Produce a 150-word reflection on time saved, quality improvements and limitations.

## Competency rubric

| Criterion | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Purpose, audience and file governance | 15 | Clear scope, owners, naming and document control |
| Spreadsheet correctness | 25 | Valid formulas, data types and totals independently verified |
| Documents, reporting and usability | 25 | Accurate, accessible communication and labelled visualisation |
| Positive and negative testing | 20 | Six test cases with objective expected and actual outcomes |
| Security, operations and reflection | 15 | Least-privilege sharing, approval route, incident and rollback plan |
| **Total** | **100** | **70/100 minimum plus all safety-critical checks** |

**Critical checks:** no actual customer data or passwords; no intentionally misleading chart; no unsupported numbers; no externally shared material without correct approval and access control. A critical failure requires remediation regardless of total score.

A qualified reviewer must verify the submitted evidence and record the decision independently before any Academy-issued completion award. A local browser score cannot substitute for that review.
