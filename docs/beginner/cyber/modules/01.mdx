# Module 1: Security fundamentals and everyday risk

**Learning time:** 45–60 minutes. **Outcome:** understand and apply this protective capability in an authorised home or small-business setting.

## Understand the threat and control

Cybersecurity protects confidentiality (only authorised people can view data), integrity (data stays accurate) and availability (services work when needed). A threat is a possible cause of harm; a vulnerability is a weakness; risk considers likelihood and impact. Identify critical assets such as accounts, payment details and business files. A control reduces risk but rarely removes it. Understand that attackers often target people and simple mistakes rather than sophisticated technical flaws.

## Practical case

A fictional café runs a shared laptop, online bookings and a payment provider. List the assets, threats, weaknesses and controls. Rank risks using a simple low/medium/high matrix.

## Guided exercise

1. List three assets and owners; mark one threat and control per asset; explain why a password on a shared account cannot guarantee traceability.
2. Create a simple table with **risk, evidence, control, owner and review date**.
3. Model a successful authorised action and an unauthorised or failed action; explain how the control responds.
4. Record observations, assumptions and recommended next steps separately.

## Avoid these mistakes

Treat a vulnerability label as an automatic incident only after investigating exposure and effects.

## Knowledge checkpoint

1. Explain the primary control and the harm it reduces.
2. What evidence would prove the control worked, and what would trigger escalation?
