# Module 2: Phishing, scams and social engineering

**Learning time:** 45–60 minutes. **Outcome:** understand and apply this protective capability in an authorised home or small-business setting.

## Understand the threat and control

Phishing attempts to trick a person into sharing information or performing an action. Smishing is via text, vishing via voice, and business email compromise impersonates an authorised colleague or supplier. Common signals include urgency, mismatched domains, changed bank details and unexpected attachments. Legitimate looking logos and HTTPS do not prove sender legitimacy. Verify unusual requests using a known independently obtained contact channel.

## Practical case

A message says 'Your manager needs you to update a supplier bank account in the next 10 minutes.' The correct response is not to reply to that message: phone the approved supplier contact from company records, apply change approval, and record the incident.

## Guided exercise

1. Inspect five simulated messages. Identify claimed sender, requested action, urgency and how to verify it without using the supplied link.
2. Create a simple table with **risk, evidence, control, owner and review date**.
3. Model a successful authorised action and an unauthorised or failed action; explain how the control responds.
4. Record observations, assumptions and recommended next steps separately.

## Avoid these mistakes

No test requires clicking a real suspicious link; use offline examples and fabricated domains such as example.invalid.

## Knowledge checkpoint

1. Explain the primary control and the harm it reduces.
2. What evidence would prove the control worked, and what would trigger escalation?
