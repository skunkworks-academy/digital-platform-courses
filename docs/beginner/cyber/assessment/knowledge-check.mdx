## Printable question version

The same questions are provided below for offline or accessible written practice. Use the interactive quiz above for immediate browser feedback; neither option issues a formal credential.

### 1. What are the three primary security objectives?

A. Confidentiality, integrity and availability  
B. Cost, speed and design  
C. Routers, printers and apps  
D. Cloud, code and cables  

### 2. How is risk usually framed?

A. Impact and likelihood  
B. Number of icons  
C. Number of devices alone  
D. Cost of internet service  

### 3. Which action is safest for changed supplier bank details?

A. Use the link in the request  
B. Confirm using an independently known contact  
C. Send the money immediately  
D. Ask the message to verify itself  

### 4. What does HTTPS prove?

A. The website is honest  
B. Traffic uses transport encryption to the site  
C. There is no phishing risk  
D. The site is owned by your employer  

### 5. Which login defence is strongest against many phishing attacks?

A. Shared short password  
B. FIDO2 passkey or security key  
C. Reuse one password  
D. Disable MFA  

### 6. Unexpected MFA pushes should be:

A. Approved quickly  
B. Denied and reported  
C. Forwarded to strangers  
D. Ignored indefinitely  

### 7. Why test backup restoration?

A. To prove files can actually be recovered  
B. To delete old data  
C. To avoid encryption  
D. To speed up Wi-Fi  

### 8. Which Wi-Fi configuration protects a business network?

A. Open guest devices on finance LAN  
B. Separate guests, update router, use WPA2/WPA3  
C. Share admin credentials  
D. Never patch routers  

### 9. Which item belongs in an incident record?

A. Time, system, facts and actions taken  
B. Only a guess about attacker identity  
C. Only the employee's opinion  
D. The account password  

### 10. What should you do with suspicious links during training?

A. Use safe offline samples  
B. Test live malicious links  
C. Download unknown files  
D. Forward to colleagues  

### 11. Least privilege means:

A. Everyone has admin access  
B. Only necessary permissions are assigned  
C. Accounts use the same password  
D. No access review  

### 12. After containing an incident, what follows?

A. Recovery validation and lessons learned  
B. Delete all evidence  
C. Disable all logs  
D. Assume it is over  

<details>
  <summary>Open marking key after all twelve responses</summary>

1. **A: Confidentiality, integrity and availability** — The CIA triad defines common protection goals.

2. **A: Impact and likelihood** — Risk concerns the chance and consequences of harm.

3. **B: Confirm using an independently known contact** — Independent out-of-band verification reduces fraud exposure.

4. **B: Traffic uses transport encryption to the site** — A scam site can have HTTPS too.

5. **B: FIDO2 passkey or security key** — Phishing-resistant authenticators resist fake sign-in pages.

6. **B: Denied and reported** — They may indicate a compromised credential.

7. **A: To prove files can actually be recovered** — Untested backups may fail in a real incident.

8. **B: Separate guests, update router, use WPA2/WPA3** — Segmenting and updating reduce exposure.

9. **A: Time, system, facts and actions taken** — An audit trail supports triage and recovery.

10. **A: Use safe offline samples** — Training must avoid unnecessary exposure.

11. **B: Only necessary permissions are assigned** — Permissions should be scoped to required work.

12. **A: Recovery validation and lessons learned** — Recovery and improvement complete the response cycle.

</details>

Score 0–9: revise the missed controls and try new examples. Score 10–12: continue to capstone. No amount of quiz progress substitutes for completing safe applied evidence and meeting the capstone rubric.
