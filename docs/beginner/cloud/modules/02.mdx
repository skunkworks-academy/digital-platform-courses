# Module 2: Understand IaaS, PaaS, SaaS and deployment options

**Time:** 50–70 minutes. **Learning criterion:** The learner shows which responsibilities remain with the customer for each service model.

## Understand the concepts

Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS) supplies virtual machines, storage and virtual networks while the customer controls operating systems, apps and data. Platform as a Service (PaaS) supplies managed application runtimes or databases while developers remain responsible for their code and data. Software as a Service (SaaS) delivers a finished application, but customer identities, permissions and information handling still matter. Public, private, hybrid and multi-cloud describe where and how resources are deployed; using two providers does not automatically provide failover. Service choice affects workload control, skill requirements, patching and cost.

## Workplace example

A small company needs hosted email, a custom bookings website and one older finance application that requires Windows administration.

## Guided practical activity

1. Match SaaS, PaaS and IaaS to the three fictional needs and explain why.
2. Fill out a shared-responsibility matrix: facility, guest operating system, application, identity and data.
3. Sketch a hybrid option linking a local office service to a cloud-hosted application.
4. Identify what would be required to make a multi-cloud failover plan real.

## Confirm the learning

The learner shows which responsibilities remain with the customer for each service model. In a table, record expected behaviour, observed or simulated behaviour, evidence, risk and next step. At least one negative test must show how an unsafe request is denied or escalated.

:::warning Safe use
Managed does not mean the customer can stop securing user accounts and data.
:::

## Self-check and reflective feedback

What are the most relevant ownership, privacy and cost controls in the scenario? Explain how you would verify the result rather than assume success.
