# Module 1: Cloud concepts and service models

**Duration:** 45–70 minutes. **Outcome:** make and justify a safe cloud design or operational decision.

## Understand the concepts

Cloud computing supplies computing resources on demand, generally over a network, with measured usage. IaaS gives infrastructure such as virtual machines and networks; PaaS manages more of the application runtime; SaaS delivers a ready-to-use application. Public, private and hybrid describe deployment and ownership contexts, not automatic levels of security. Elasticity, availability, global regions and usage-based pricing can improve operations but require governance. Responsibility for data and configuration never disappears merely because a provider runs the hardware.

## Worked scenario

A business currently hosts a brochure website on a local desktop. Compare a managed web service, a virtual machine and a fully managed website builder, listing cost, administration effort and control.

## Guided practical

1. Make a service-model table with customer responsibilities for identity, application, data, patches and physical facilities. Select the least complex model that meets the hypothetical requirements.
2. Draw or tabulate the intended resource, people and trust boundaries.
3. Identify at least one measurable success test, one failure/denial test and their expected outcomes.
4. Record your test result or fully specified simulation, then explain a corrective action if it fails.
5. Present a 100-word recommendation including costs, risk and responsibility.

## Implementation caution

Do not assume every cloud solution is cheaper, compliant or more secure than on-premises hosting; verify requirements.

## Check understanding

- Which concern belongs to the cloud provider, and which remains the customer's responsibility here?
- How could you verify the result without incurring a bill or exposing private information?

<details>
  <summary>Review the expected response</summary>

Credit a response that matches responsibilities to service models, states explicit cost/security assumptions and gives an independently reproducible test. A vendor promise or dashboard image alone does not prove operational readiness.
