# Module 6: Deployment, monitoring and recovery

**Duration:** 45–70 minutes. **Outcome:** make and justify a safe cloud design or operational decision.

## Understand the concepts

A deployment lifecycle covers source, build or configuration, controlled release, validation, rollback and ongoing ownership. Monitor availability, response errors, latency, usage and spend; metrics and alerts need a named responder and useful thresholds. Define recovery time objective (RTO) and recovery point objective (RPO) separately. RTO targets maximum time to recover, while RPO targets acceptable data loss. Run a harmless simulated outage test and confirm recovery dependencies before declaring readiness.

## Worked scenario

A fictional service wants recovery within four hours and at most one hour of data loss. Record RTO=4h and RPO=1h; then evaluate whether daily backup alone can satisfy the RPO.

## Guided practical

1. Create a deployment runbook: prerequisites, approvals, health checks, rollback, backup test, incident contact and post-release follow-up.
2. Draw or tabulate the intended resource, people and trust boundaries.
3. Identify at least one measurable success test, one failure/denial test and their expected outcomes.
4. Record your test result or fully specified simulation, then explain a corrective action if it fails.
5. Present a 100-word recommendation including costs, risk and responsibility.

## Implementation caution

Cloud replication without a tested restore strategy does not satisfy a data recovery objective.

## Check understanding

- Which concern belongs to the cloud provider, and which remains the customer's responsibility here?
- How could you verify the result without incurring a bill or exposing private information?

Credit a response that matches responsibilities to service models, states explicit cost/security assumptions and gives an independently reproducible test. A vendor promise or dashboard image alone does not prove operational readiness.
