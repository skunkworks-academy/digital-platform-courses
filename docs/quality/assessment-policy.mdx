# Assessment Policy

## Assessment model

Each course uses three layers: ungraded module checkpoints, guided practical labs, and a summative capstone. The knowledge check confirms conceptual understanding; the capstone confirms integrated performance in a realistic context.

| Decision element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Knowledge threshold | At least 80% |
| Capstone threshold | At least 70/100 overall |
| Critical criteria | Every safety/security criterion must be met |
| Evidence | Authentic, attributable, relevant, sufficient, and redacted |
| Retakes | Two guided retries after feedback; a materially equivalent scenario is used |
| Currency | Assessment reviewed at least annually and after material platform changes |
| Appeals | Learner submits criterion, evidence, and reason; a second competent reviewer decides |

## Fairness and reliability

Assessors use the published analytic rubric and record criterion-level scores. Conflicts of interest are disclosed. A sample of decisions is moderated by a second reviewer. Any automated or AI-supported scoring is advisory: a competent human retains the decision and checks anomalous outcomes.

## Evidence retention and privacy

The learner owns original work. The Academy should retain only the minimum evidence needed under its published privacy and retention schedule. Secrets, tokens, customer records, payment data, private code, and unnecessary personal information are prohibited in submissions. Access to assessment records must be role-based and auditable.

## Achievement language

Successful learners may receive a course completion statement or Academy badge that names the assessed outcomes, issue date, version, and verification method. It must not be described as ISO certification, accreditation, professional licensure, or official GitHub, CodeRabbit, or Shopify certification unless separately authorized by the relevant body.
